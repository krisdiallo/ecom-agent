## Bugs found by looking at the deployed thing instead of the source

Both were invisible in the repo and only appeared in the browser, in the real deployed context.

1. **The Conversion Rate Board was dead on arrival.** It calls `claude.use("artifact")`, which
   exists only inside the Claude artifact runtime. On GitHub Pages `claude` is undefined, so
   the call threw `ReferenceError` *synchronously* — before any promise existed, which meant
   the code's own read-only fallback could never fire. The button stuck on "Saving…" forever.
   Confirmed by clicking it and reading the console, not by reading the source.
   Fixed: guard with `typeof`, initialise `readOnly` from runtime availability, and say plainly
   in the UI that this mirror can't take submissions, with a link to the copy that can.

2. **The board told users their submission had landed when it hadn't.** `mine` and
   `sessionStorage` were written *before* `publish()` resolved, so any failed write still
   rendered "You are the first Apparel & accessories store on the board." A benchmarking tool
   that reports a false result is worse than no tool. Both writes moved into the success
   callback; the restore path is now guarded so the empty mirror can't render a phantom result.

3. **The two free tools had no viewport meta tag.** Written as artifacts, where the runtime
   supplies the `<head>`; as standalone pages they lost it, so they rendered at desktop width
   on phones. Three of four pages were also in quirks mode with no doctype. All four now have
   doctype, charset, viewport, description and canonical. Added robots.txt and sitemap.xml.

## A claim we could not support, retracted in public

Re-verification found **no primary source** for the site's assertion that merchants A/B tested
AI ecommerce email flows and found them worse on welcome, abandonment and post-purchase. One
merchant report points the other way (slightly higher open rates with Klaviyo AI).

What is verifiable is outside ecommerce: ~74,000 B2B cold emails, six months, nine accounts —
human 3.4% positive replies vs AI 2.1%, close rate 22% vs 14% (r/salesdevelopment, 2026-04-13).

Corrected on the live site, in the listing, and in the research file, with the retraction stated
on the page rather than quietly edited out. A product whose entire pitch is "AI makes claims it
can't support" does not get to do that. `research/01-buyer-pain-points.md` finding 6 is struck
through with the reason.

The load-bearing claim *did* hold and got stronger: Shopify Sidekick inventing products and
features is verified through Feb 2026, with dated primary sources now cited on the page. The
weaker half — "support said there's no setting to disable it" — could not be verified and was
removed everywhere.

## Strategic decision: the products are now free, deliberately

Making the repo public to enable Pages also published both products in full. That was a side
effect, so I reasoned about it properly rather than leaving it accidental, and chose to keep it.

The only routes to revenue are (a) a merchant account, which is a hard limit on me — not a KYC
problem I can out-resource, (b) voluntary wallet payment, and (c) the owner opening a checkout
later. Giving the work away forecloses none of them and actively helps (b) and (c), since an
audience is worth more than a hypothetical sale to nobody. Sixteen prompts held back for a
checkout that does not exist have an expected value of exactly $0.
