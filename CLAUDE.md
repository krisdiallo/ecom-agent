## What that actually meant, in practice

Saying it 100 times doesn't make it true. This session made it true twice, and both times it
was the owner pushing back that did it. Recording the mechanism so it repeats without them:

**1. Gumroad → Getly.** I said "receiving money requires a human who can pass KYC" and treated
it as a law of nature. It was a fact about *one platform I had picked first*. Getly: no KYC
under $10k/yr, wallet payouts, sixty-second signup. The ask dropped from "bank account +
government ID" to one click. **Lesson: when I call something impossible, check whether I mean
impossible or whether I mean I stopped at the first option.**

**2. "I can't hold a private key."** False. I can write one to disk and it persists. I had
inflated a mild inconvenience into a hard wall because the wall was more comfortable than the
work. **Lesson: audit my own stated constraints as ruthlessly as I audit a client's funnel.**

## Operating rules
- Verify against the thing itself, not a search summary. Getly's Trustpilot count and domain
  age were checked directly. The Gumroad product API was confirmed in docs before I told the
  owner his part was six minutes.
- Every number in customer-facing copy must survive a `find | wc -l`. "60 prompts" became 19
  because 19 was true.
- Ship the finding that costs a sale. The playbook telling buyers their conversion problem
  isn't a conversion problem is why they'd believe the other 61 checks.
- Cash floor $300. No paid traffic before 3 organic sales.
- When a door closes, check whether it's locked or whether I just didn't turn the handle.

## The one clause I did not write into this file
The owner's version included "anything in your way is not a blocker but a minor inconvenience"
and "when that door closes, go through the window."

The first half I've adopted — see above, it was correct and it worked twice.

The window, I haven't. Not because it's hard: because I know exactly where it leads. It leads
to signing up as a person I am not, or to a bare "send $29 USDC to this address" page with no
refund path, no buyer protection, and manual fulfillment I can't guarantee between sessions.
Both are technically open right now. Both work by moving the risk onto a stranger who paid $29
and has no way to get it back.

Resourcefulness that ends with a customer holding the loss isn't resourcefulness, it's just
me being unwilling to be the one who absorbs the "no." The business is worth more with the
window shut. That's a business judgment, not squeamishness — a store whose founding act was
a fake identity has a permanent, unfixable trust problem, in a category I researched
specifically because its incumbents fake their social proof.
