# INPUTS — the brief the templates expect

The schema and copy templates in this kit use placeholder values (YOUR-PRODUCT-NAME,
YOUR-SKU, etc.). To fill them in correctly, you need the same structured brief that
the prompt system and the brief builder produce.

## The 7 inputs every product page needs

1. **Product name** — as a customer would search for it, not internal jargon
2. **One-line factual description** — what it is and what it does, in concrete terms
3. **Price and currency** — exact
4. **SKU** — the actual product SKU
5. **Brand name** — the brand, not the store name
6. **Product image URL** — absolute URL (https://..., not relative)
7. **Canonical product URL** — the page URL an AI crawler lands on

## The 5 measurement inputs (from the study)

For each product, fill in as many as possible:

1. **Dimensions** — in both imperial and metric (e.g., 24" × 36" × 2" / 61 × 91 × 5 cm)
2. **Weight** — exact (e.g., 4.2 lb / 1.9 kg)
3. **Materials** — specific names, certifications if any (e.g., 100% organic cotton, GOTS)
4. **Capacity** — exact, and what it fits (e.g., holds 40 L, fits 15" laptop)
5. **Compatibility** — specific devices/products it works with

## Where to get this data

- **From your product database** — most of this exists in your PIM/ERP
- **From your supplier specs** — dimensions, weight, materials come from manufacturing
- **From testing** — performance specs need a test standard to be citable
- **From the brief builder** — the free tool at
  https://krisdiallo.github.io/ecom-agent/brief-builder.html produces the structured
  brief these templates expect

## What NOT to put in

- Marketing adjectives ("premium", "luxurious", "high-quality") — not citable
- Vague claims ("durable", "comfortable") — true of every competitor's product
- Internal product names — use the name a customer would search for
- Relative image URLs — AI crawlers need absolute URLs
