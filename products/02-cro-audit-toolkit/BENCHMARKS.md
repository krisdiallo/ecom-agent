# Benchmarks — what normal looks like

Read this first. Half of all "my store is broken" panic is a store performing normally, and
half of all complacency is a store performing terribly against a number nobody looked up.

## Use these as ranges, not targets
Published ecommerce benchmarks vary widely by source, vertical, traffic mix and year, and
most vendor-published numbers are flattering to the vendor. Treat everything below as an
order-of-magnitude sanity check, not a precise standard. **Your own trend over time is a far
better signal than any industry average** — a store going 1.2% → 1.6% is winning regardless
of what the average is.

| Metric | Rough range | What it means when you're below it |
|---|---|---|
| Sitewide conversion rate | ~1–3% typical; 3%+ is strong | Look at traffic quality before page copy |
| Product page → add to cart | ~5–10% | The page isn't answering a buying question |
| Cart → checkout start | ~50–70% | Shipping cost or account-required friction |
| Checkout start → purchase | ~50–80% | Payment options, trust, or surprise costs |
| Mobile CR vs desktop | Mobile usually lower | A gap larger than ~40% is a mobile-specific bug |
| Email flow revenue share | Often 20–30% of total | You have no flows, or they're not connected |
| Return rate | Varies enormously by category | Above your category norm = a copy/spec problem |

## The one number that matters most
**Mobile product-page conversion rate.** It's where most traffic lands and where most stores
are worst. If you audit one surface, audit that one.

## An honest warning about benchmarks
A store at 0.8% with the wrong traffic and a store at 0.8% with a broken checkout need
opposite interventions. The number tells you to look; it never tells you where.
