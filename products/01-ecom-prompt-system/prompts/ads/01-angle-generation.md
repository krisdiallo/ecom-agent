# Ad Angles (before you write a single headline)

**Use when:** creative is fatiguing, or you're launching cold traffic. **Output:** 8 angles, ranked.

---

## Prompt

```
Generate advertising angles. An angle is not a headline — it's a claim about why this
product matters to a specific person at a specific moment. Headlines come after.

[STORE BRIEF]

PRODUCT:
WHAT'S ALREADY RUNNING (paste current ads + their CTR/CPA if known):
WHAT HAS FAILED BEFORE (and the metric that said so):

Produce 8 angles across these families — do not give me 8 versions of one family:
1. Problem-agitate (the pain before the product)
2. Mechanism (why THIS works when the last thing didn't)
3. Identity (who you become / who already uses it)
4. Comparison (vs the alternative they're using now, including doing nothing)
5. Objection-first (lead with the reason they'd say no)
6. Proof-led (open with the number or the review)
7. Occasion (the moment that triggers purchase)
8. Contrarian (the accepted advice in this category that's wrong)

For each: the angle in one sentence, the exact buyer it targets, the belief they must
already hold for it to land, and the single strongest hook line.

Then RANK them by expected performance for cold traffic and explain the top choice's
reasoning in two sentences.

RULES:
- No unsubstantiated superiority or health claims. Flag anything needing legal review.
- Proof-led angles may only cite proof from the brief. No invented statistics.
- If the product genuinely has no differentiation, say so — that is a product problem
  and no angle will fix it.
```

---

## Why angles before headlines
Most "give me 20 headlines" prompts return 20 rewrites of one idea. You learn nothing from
testing them, because they all fail or win together. Testing across angle families is how
you find out *which claim* works; only then is it worth generating variations inside it.
